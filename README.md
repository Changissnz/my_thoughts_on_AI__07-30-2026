# My Thoughts on AI: 07-30-2026

Another opinion piece written by me on AI and related topics surrounding the politics of production. 
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is my third opinion piece concerning the artificial intelligence industry, mainly that is. In my 
first opinion [piece](https://github.com/Changissnz/my_thoughts_on_AI__07-05-2026), I stated how certain 
emerging practices spawned by LLM technology are here to stay, and that regressing back towards 1990s/2000s 
norms of technocracy will do no good even if there seems to be valid reason to do so, i.e. going back 
towards the "roots", the "homeland", or sympathizing with certain immigrant groups that have more than 
enough hands and bodies to do a good fraction of the labor and more, groups that do not see the intellectual 
value of artificial intelligence development or might even be existentially threatened by it if worse comes 
to worse. I also wrote a little on the economic disparity that artificial intelligence is helping to produce, 
how economies can be destabilized and the begrudging necessity of classism as societal sorting mechanisms, 
if not also capable of becoming divisive ones. In my second opinion [piece](https://github.com/Changissnz/my_thoughts_on_AI__07-17-2026), 
I shared my opinion on the shadowy power of financial speculation, namely the character of Masayoshi Son and his 
"revelation" that there is no artificial intelligence bubble, which then is a statement that growing economic problems 
of disparities and inequalities are NOT because of developments in artificial intelligence. I guess the man might be 
right, but he issues his statements out of the bottom of his financial interests (Korea, Manchuria, and Japan I presume), 
not because he is the wisest and error-free financier ever in the Eastern hemisphere. I also used words such 
as "intelligentsia" in that discussion surrounding artificial intelligence because that word is very complementary 
to the industry. Cultural expectations, as I tried to elucidate in that article, are strange in that they are 
conservative at "source", or at "poverty" perhaps is the better choice of word, and become represented as such 
incredulity and questionable strength through the course of deviation by aspiration. I mentioned works such as 
"Scarface" and writers such as Mario Puzo in not-so-commending ways because I was trying to get at the point 
that the anti-ideal is actually just as hard to achieve as the ideal, in metaphysical terms. I felt it important 
to express those things because it lets me say what I think the zeros and anti-zeros are, what with inflationary 
measures and financial engineering surrounding artificial intelligence, which I mentioned somewhere in my previous 
writings is actually quite state-sponsored. Yes, that is right. There is just no way common businesses can just 
develop large-scale artificial intelligence systems. It's an extensive ecosystem of databases and foreign-sourced 
hardware. Back before computers, it was called economic and logistics planning, done on paper first by scribes and 
similar types working for the government. That worked out fairly well in autocratic societies where most people were 
illiterate and obediently domesticated. It had to have been both, not one or the other, for the plannings and predictions 
to have worked out well. Of course, in this "post-modern" world, I wouldn't go around and tell every person on the street, 
"hey, you know the thing with artificial intelligence? It's actually government business, essentially. Artificial intelligence 
business is corporatist and you should really just fall in line or get labeled a regressive Easterner working for the communist 
blocs." That would be ludicrous and insulting of me, wouldn't it? And people would get demoralized, some of them falling 
into such deep insecurity at the circumstances surrounding their times that they might... you know... when reason goes out the 
door or gets done by machines because they don't make mistakes. And let's not forget, if artificial intelligence is government 
business ("federal"), you know a lot of local communities would hate it, as seen by these staged demonstrations against 
data centers, politics that has added more to the stains riddling us all, from politics to engineering to society people 
to the...wait for it, the most important word ever... the populace. It's that kind of consideration going on nowadays. 

Artificial intelligence industry is partly done out of the comfort of luxury, and mostly for luxurious ends. This is one 
claim that I would like to promote. I'm talking about the development of the activity of machine intelligence, not the 
hardware infrastructure. Now the hardware infrastructure is based on principles going back decades since around the steam 
engine. It is hard work, expensive factories needed to be erected, and is, from what I can gather, turning into a modern-day 
sword-crafting competition, something that somewhat disappoints me because everyone knows what sword-crafting leads to. The 
process of crafting swords is fun and all but for what ends but...fill in the blank please. There are a lot of funky ideas, 
and some of those ideas require kinds of madnesses that only luxury, not the grit of single-minded discipline to working for 
some prescribed form, can produce. With artificial intelligence, I don't think some of the more important developments can 
be forced. It is a frontier into studies on applied metaphysics, and has been and will be prone to kooky cultural practices, 
cultisms and such due to cultural preferences extending beyond the universal language of mathematics. 

I think the honeymoon phase behind this latest wave of artificial intelligence developments might already be over for many 
people involved. One of the latest fads is something called "loop engineering", pushed by one of Anthropic Corporation's founders. 
This "loop engineering" is the sequel to "prompt engineering", an automation of prompting a generative tool. This "loop engineering" 
thing will probably force some more people out of the payrolls. It sounds to me that these systems can actually start feeding on 
their own outputs without noticeable detriment, having gained enough high-quality data to be sufficient enough for tasks going so 
far as discovering a flaw in Fermat's Last Theorem. I was introduced to that theorem in my undergraduate years and the flaw uncovered 
by Anthropic's systems completely went over my head. Looking back, I was actually somewhat dull and inattentive to a lot of matters 
during that time. So yes... people such as me definitely cannot go up against A.I. brains that easily. One v One is definitely no 
longer something feasible, ever since Google's AlphaGo system. 

The goalpost will just keep on moving further and further along. Some people will find time to play a little in these developments. 
Times will be challenging to maintain interest and enthusiasm for continuing advancing this field. And the politics involved 
will oftentimes divest capital and spin matters out to be something else. And I also think institutional integrity is important 
for continuing the A.I. advances. And that translates to a de-emphasis on elements found in free market practices as well as 
bureaucratic management. The elements in both of these managerial styles can be very deterministic. I assume this because free 
market practices will inevitably favor the rich, at least financially, until the market crashes. The market always does, from time to time. 
Bureaucratic management leads to sloth and disinterest over time and common sense says it's because of the incentive structure and 
for the better of common cause. The word, institutions, is a funny word, from my perspective. It is as if anything that goes 
according to plan, to societal expectations, is institutionally valid and falls under the legitimacy of institutions, right? Yes 
or no? So there's no way to make moves without the backing of institutions. It is truisms such as this that make some time periods 
of American Republican administration, since 2016, humorous in the hypocritical conservative-knows-best kind of fashion. In the timespan 
of 2020-2023, the metaphorical wrecking balls to the economy came. Personally, I think some economic sectors, especially those outside 
of white-collar work, fared well enough during that time. But the wrecking balls to the economy really were an eye-opener for many people. 
The policy shifts leave behind what is "essential", the "core institutions". And then some people started to realize that the United 
States, among other developed nations, has been sitting on top of a lot of dysfunction, not thrones, going on around the world. 
Not cooperating is a human right, right? Feeding illusions and depriving resources? All part of the game...and it only needs a new 
name to continue on as something more...something less. These times do send strong messages out to observant people on how much 
unofficialdom bears impact. Some people want the gates to remain open but are incapable of entering the premises or the gates are 
part of some sinister mirage, a test for those trying to prove that some things actually are real or can actually occur. Outsourcing 
manufacturing worked out well because the trade exchanges started out equal in the eyes of the nations shaking hands to the terms. One 
side focuses on hardware, the other side software, for example. But then the demands for advancements, due to the corporate ladder, 
started to budge under pressure. Russia, for one, has not been welcoming of American corporatism for decades now, and it shows in the 
statements issued by their government officials, understandable since people were getting cut out of these exclusionary lucrative 
arrangements. 

A last note I would like to make is that these changes, along with artificial intelligence trends, provide a great learning lesson 
for people from all parts of the political spectrum. The lesson is "play along but do not expect too much". Another related lesson 
is "there is a time to stop pretending, so try to not pretend too much". Expectations can be dashed. There are limits and ends. And 
oftentimes, the image is way way better than the pragmatics that arise to substantiate the claims and expectations.
